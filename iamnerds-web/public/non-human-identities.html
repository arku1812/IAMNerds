Non-human identity & AI agents

Not every identity has a pulse.

Machine identities now outnumber people in most environments, and AI agents are joining them fast. They log in, call APIs and touch production data. We govern them with the same discipline as your employees.

Four rules for every machine identity

Whether it is a ten-year-old service account or an AI agent deployed last week, the same four questions decide whether it is under control.

  • OwnerEvery service account and agent has a named, accountable human. When that person leaves, ownership moves, it doesn't vanish.
  • PurposeAccess is tied to a documented job, not to "it might need it". No purpose, no access.
  • ScopeLeast privilege by default, with secrets vaulted and rotated instead of hard-coded in scripts.
  • ExpiryCredentials and permissions end when the job does, and are reviewed regularly in between.

What we bring under governance

Non-human identities hide in many places. We start by finding them.

ACCOUNTS

Service accounts

Technical accounts in Active Directory, Entra ID, SAP and databases, many of them with no owner and far too many rights.

SECRETS

API keys & secrets

Keys, tokens and passwords in code, pipelines and config files, moved into a vault and rotated.

WORKLOADS

Workload identities

Cloud workloads, containers and managed identities with scoped permissions instead of broad ones.

AI AGENTS

AI agents

Agents acting on behalf of users or teams, with delegated, time-bound access and a full activity trail.

How we start

A practical first project that shows results in weeks, not quarters.

  1. Inventory

    Discover service accounts, secrets and agents across directories, cloud and key applications.

  2. Assign

    Give every identity a named owner and a documented purpose. Flag the orphans.

  3. Reduce

    Remove unused accounts, cut excessive rights and vault the secrets.

  4. Govern

    Add machine identities to recertification and set expiry by default.

Frequently asked questions

What is a non-human identity?

Any identity that is not a person: service accounts, API keys, secrets, certificates, workload identities, bots and AI agents.

Why do AI agents need identity governance?

AI agents act with real permissions on real systems. Without an owner, a defined scope and an expiry, they become the next generation of orphaned, over-privileged accounts.

Can this work with our existing IGA platform?

Usually yes. Most IGA platforms can manage non-human identities and their owners. We extend what you have before suggesting anything new.
